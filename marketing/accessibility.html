Accessibility

Designed for access, not just appearance.

Symptom Story aims to remain calm and usable across different bodies, senses, devices, and difficult days.

Built into daily tasks

  • Large touch targets and primary actions placed within comfortable reach.
  • Programmatic headings, field labels, button roles, and selected or checked states.
  • Plain-language summaries alongside every trend visualization.
  • Keyboard-aware forms and visible focus indicators on the website.
  • Safe-area support, flexible scrolling, and layouts that adapt to text and screen size.
  • Motion that respects the operating system’s Reduce Motion preference.

Language and cognitive load

Questions are short, optional, and presented one at a time. The experience avoids guilt, medical jargon, and decorative complexity when someone may have limited attention or energy.

Our ongoing commitment

Automated semantics are not a substitute for testing with people. Screen-reader, switch-control, voice-control, dynamic-text, contrast, and cognitive-accessibility testing remain part of release validation.
